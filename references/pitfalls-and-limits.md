# Pitfalls and limits

## Contents
- Anti-pattern checklist
- Limits and how to adapt

## Anti-pattern checklist

Use it to review a plan or a past round of discovery.

**Planning**
- No learning goal, or a goal no answer could change a decision on
- Asking people for what you could look up
- Research done to prove yourself right, or for show
- Convenience samples; a segment that is too broad
- Invented personas instead of observed behavior

**Interviews**
- Asking what to build; hypotheticals ("would you..."); asking for opinions on your idea
- Pitching, or fishing for compliments
- Treating "I'd definitely buy that" as validation
- Digging into detail before confirming the problem matters; ignoring signs of indifference
- One person doing all interviews and filtering the findings
- Interviewing only when a project needs it
- Sharing raw notes instead of a snapshot, or only conclusions without quotes

**Mapping**
- Opportunities worded from the company's view
- Overlapping branches; one opportunity with two parents; long single-child chains
- Solutions disguised as opportunities; feelings listed as opportunities

**Ideation**
- Variations of a single idea; a single ideation session
- Group-first brainstorming; no diverse voices
- Committing to one idea early

**Assumptions and tests**
- Testing ideas instead of assumptions; too few assumptions; negatively phrased ones
- Vague success criteria, or percentages instead of counts; criteria set after seeing results
- Wrong audience; best-case-only tests
- Proving value with a mock-up
- Trusting charts or surveys without enough data
- Using surveys or experiments instead of talking to people

**Decisions and sharing**
- Waiting for more data before deciding a reversible choice
- Relying on a single factor; working backward from a preferred answer
- Leading stakeholder conversations with solutions
- Debating ideas instead of showing evidence; overwhelming people with detail

**Process**
- Treating discovery as a one-time phase before delivery
- Bringing engineers in only to estimate
- Months of conversations with no testing

## Limits and how to adapt

- **Discovery lowers the odds of failure; it does not remove them.** Stay ready to be wrong, and treat every conclusion as the current best guess.
- **Interview stories can be coherent and still mislead.** Cross-check them against observed behavior and tests.
- **Interviews test the customer side only.** Technical risk needs a feasibility prototype.
- **Brand-new products with no customers yet.** You cannot interview users you do not have. Start with explicit hypotheses about the problem, the segment and the value; recruit prospects; and test demand with cheap signals such as landing pages and pre-orders. Then move to the weekly interview habit.
- **Small teams or little support.** Begin with one interview a week and shared notes. Do the lightest version of each step.
- **Large organizations.** Many of the examples in the literature come from big consumer technology firms. If you have strict approval or compliance processes, move the test ladder toward low-risk methods first (interviews, prototype tests) and involve legal and security early.
- **Regulated or hard-to-reach customers.** Use a small reference group, introductions and observation. Plan extra time for recruiting.
- **Weekly interviewing is demanding.** Automate recruiting and scheduling so effort doesn't depend on one person.
- **A poor outcome undermines everything.** Check the outcome with stakeholders and make sure the team can actually influence it.
